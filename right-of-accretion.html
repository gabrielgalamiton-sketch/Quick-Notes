📜 Important Doctrinal Evolution (Spanish Code vs. New Civil Code Art. 1017):
Under the Old Spanish Civil Code, accretion required "no special designation of parts" (sin especial designación de partes), meaning unequal fractional shares (e.g., 1/3 to A and 2/3 to B) barred accretion. Under Article 1017 of the New Civil Code (Majority View: Balane, Jurado, Caguioa, Paras), accretion DOES take place even when shares are unequal fractions, provided they refer to aliquot parts of an undivided whole (pro indiviso) resulting in co-ownership and do not physically earmark determinate property to each heir.

PART I — Concept, Nature, Rationale & Testamentary Requisites

1. Concept, Nature, Rationale, and Causes of Accretion (Art. 1015)

Imagine a testator who leaves a beachfront vacation house jointly to his three closest college friends, A, B, and C. Before the testator dies, friend A passes away, leaving A's one-third share vacant.

Under Article 1015, accretion (jus accrescendi) is a right by virtue of which, when two or more persons are called to the same inheritance, devise, or legacy, the part assigned to the one who renounces, cannot receive his share, or died before the testator is added or incorporated into that of his co-heirs, co-devisees, or co-legatees. It is an incidental right, not an obligation—the surviving co-beneficiary remains free to accept or repudiate the accruing portion.

Rationale in Testate Succession

Presumed Will of the Testator: The law presumes the deceased prefers to give the conjoint property or quota entirely to the designated group rather than let a vacant fraction fall into general intestacy.

Rationale in Intestate Succession

Presumed Equal Affection: The Code Commission noted that relatives called conjointly by law occupy substantially the same position in the decedent's affection.

Memory aid: Causes of Vacancy — P – I – R (+ C – I)
Five causes that create a vacant share eligible for accretion, and two ways a testator can exclude accretion.
PPredeceaseThe heir, legatee, or devisee dies before the testator.
IIncapacity / UnworthinessThe successor is legally disqualified or declared unworthy to inherit.
RRepudiationThe successor formally refuses or renounces his share (Note: disinheritance is excluded because it applies only to compulsory heirs' legitimes).
+ CCondition UnfulfilledNon-fulfillment of a suspensive condition (treated as conditional incapacity).
+ IIdentification FailureWhen one co-heir cannot be identified, though the other co-instituted heirs are clearly identified.

How Testator Excludes Accretion (2 Ways): (1) By Express Substitution (appointing an express substitute under Art. 859 overrides implied accretion); or (2) By Express Prohibition (explicitly stating in the will that no accretion shall occur).

2. Requisites for Accretion in Testamentary Succession (Art. 1016)

Imagine two sisters, Maria and Clara, who are named together in their aunt's will to inherit a single parcel of commercial land. If Maria renounces her share so she can emigrate abroad without property management duties, Clara wants to know if she automatically gets the whole land.

Under Article 1016 (as synthesized by Manresa, Paras, and Jurado), testamentary accretion requires four cumulative requisites: (1) Plurality of subjects called to inherit, (2) Unity of object (conjoint institution pro indiviso to the same inheritance or portion), (3) a Vacant portion due to predecease, incapacity, or repudiation, and (4) Acceptance of the accruing share by the surviving co-successor.

Memory aid: P – U – V – A (4 Cumulative Requisites of Art. 1016)
Arrow Formula: PLURALITY OF HEIRS → PRO INDIVISO OBJECT → VACANT SHARE → ACCEPTANCE
PPlurality of SubjectsTwo or more persons (heirs, legatees, or devisees) are called to inherit.
UUnity of ObjectCalled conjointly to a single inheritance, determinate property, or undivided quota pro indiviso.
VVacant PortionOne of those called dies before the testator, repudiates, or is incapacitated.
AAcceptanceThe surviving co-successor accepts both the original share and the accruing portion.

3. Meaning of Pro Indiviso & the Earmarking Test (Art. 1017)

Imagine a father who owns a 3-door apartment building and two bank accounts. Does leaving "1/3 each" of the apartment to his three nephews have the same legal effect as leaving "Door 1 to A, Door 2 to B, and Door 3 to C"?

Under Article 1017, the words "one-half for each" or "in equal shares" or any designation of an aliquot part do not exclude accretion, so long as they do not make each heir the exclusive owner of determinate property. For money or fungible goods, accretion applies whenever the share of each beneficiary is not earmarked (not physically segregated).

Symmetric Contrast: The Earmarking Test (Art. 1017)

ALIQUOT QUOTA = ACCRETION APPLIES  |  PHYSICAL SEGREGATION (EARMARKED) = NO ACCRETION

Asset Type ✓ Accretion Granted (Pro Indiviso) ✗ Accretion Excluded (Earmarked)
Real Property "I leave my 3-door apartment building to A, B, and C in equal undivided shares" (or "1/3 to A and 2/3 to B"). Co-ownership is created over the whole building. "I leave Door 1 of my apartment to A, Door 2 to B, and Door 3 to C" (or "northern half to A, southern half to B"). Each is exclusive owner of a distinct unit.
Money / Fungible Goods "₱1 Million bank deposit to X and Y in 1/2 share each" or "₱30,000 to A and ₱40,000 to B out of my general cash." Drawn from a single unsegregated fund. "Money in the left drawer to A, money in the right drawer to B" or "₱500k in Land Bank to A and ₱300k in safety deposit box to B." Physically segregated sources.
⚠️ The Favorite Examination Trap — Unequal Fractional Shares (1/3 vs. 2/3):
Examiners love to ask: "Testator leaves his parcel of land 1/4 to A and 3/4 to B. A predeceases the testator. Does A's 1/4 accrue to B?" Do not fall for the obsolete Spanish Civil Code rule! Under the New Civil Code (Art. 1017), unequal fractions (1/4 and 3/4) are still aliquot parts held pro indiviso in co-ownership. Because neither A nor B was made the exclusive owner of a physically segregated portion, accretion takes place and B gets the entire land.

PART II — Intestate Accretion, Proportional Sharing & The Legitime

4. Accretion in Legal or Intestate Succession (Art. 1018)

Imagine a decedent who dies without a will, survived by three brothers—A, B, and C—and A's son (nephew N). If brother A formally renounces his inheritance hoping his son N will step into his shoes, what happens to A's share?

Under Article 1018, "in legal succession the share of the person who repudiates the inheritance shall always accrue to his co-heirs." Because an heir who renounces can never be represented (Art. 977), A's repudiated share accrues directly to his co-heirs B and C, completely excluding nephew N.

How the 3 Causes of Vacancy Operate in Intestacy
Cause in Intestacy Primary Rule When Accretion / Own Right Applies
1. Repudiation Always triggers accretion (Art. 1018) because a renouncer cannot be represented (Art. 977). Accrues to co-heirs in the same degree (unless all in the same degree repudiate, calling the next degree in their own right under Art. 969).
2. Incapacity Triggers Right of Representation first if qualified representatives exist. Accretion occurs only when representation cannot take place (e.g., no descendants, or in the collateral line beyond nephews/nieces under Art. 972).
3. Predecease Triggers Right of Representation first if qualified representatives exist. If no representatives exist, the surviving co-heirs inherit the entire estate in their own right (per capita).

Juridical Note (Justice J.B.L. Reyes vs. Code Commission): Justice J.B.L. Reyes argued Art. 1018 is technically superfluous because intestate co-heirs take a vacant share in their own right by operation of law. The Code Commission (and Manresa, Tolentino, Caguioa, Paras) retained Art. 1018 based on presumed equal affection—and noted the practical mathematical distribution is identical.

Modification 1: Concurrence with Representation (By Root)

When grandchildren inherit by representation (dividing their predeceased parent's root/stirpes), if one grandchild repudiates, his vacant share accrues exclusively to his co-representatives (his siblings) within that root—not to his uncles or aunts.

Modification 2: Division by Lines in Ascendants (Art. 987)

In the ascending direct line, the estate first splits 50–50 between paternal and maternal lines. If the maternal grandmother repudiates, her share accrues exclusively to the maternal grandfather, not to the paternal grandparents.

5. Proportional Sharing & Transmission of Obligations (Arts. 1019–1020)

Imagine Testator X leaves a ₱1.2 Million estate to four relatives in unequal fractions: A (1/2), B (1/4), C (1/8), and D (1/8). When A renounces his ₱600,000 half, do B, C, and D split that ₱600,000 equally at ₱200,000 each, or in proportion to their initial shares?

Under Article 1019, the heirs to whom the portion goes by accretion take it in the same proportion that they inherit (analogous to reciprocal substitution under Art. 861). Furthermore, under Article 1020, accretion operates cum onere—the accruing heirs succeed to all the rights and obligations which the renouncing or incapacitated heir would have had, unless the testator expressly provided otherwise or the obligation is intuitu personae (purely personal).

6. Accretion Among Compulsory Heirs & The Legitime (Art. 1021)

Imagine a wealthy father with a ₱1.2 Million estate who is survived by two legitimate children (A and B), his wife (W), and an illegitimate child (X). In his will, he gives them their strict legitimes and leaves the remaining ₱150,000 free portion to all four of them equally. If child A repudiates his entire inheritance, why must we split A's share into two different legal buckets?

Under Article 1021, NO ACCRETION CAN EVER TAKE PLACE WITH RESPECT TO THE LEGITIME. Among compulsory heirs, accretion occurs only within the disposable free portion when left conjointly to two or more of them, or to any one of them and a stranger. If the part repudiated is the legitime, the other co-heirs succeed to it in their own right (by intestate ratios), and not by accretion.

Why the distinction matters in the Bar Exam: A's ₱300k legitime follows the 2:2:1 statutory ratio, whereas A's ₱37.5k free-portion share follows the 1:1:1 testamentary ratio!

PART III — Hierarchy of Vacancies, Particular Titles & Acceptance Rules

7. The ISRAI Hierarchy of Filling Vacant Shares (Art. 1022)

Imagine a probate judge looking at a vacant testamentary share. The decedent's grandchild claims it by representation, a co-heir claims it by accretion, a named substitute claims it by substitution, and a legal heir claims it by intestacy. Who wins first?

Under Article 1022, in testamentary succession, when the right of accretion does not take place and no substitute has been designated, the vacant portion passes to the legal heirs of the testator, who receive it with the same charges and obligations. Courts resolve competing claims strictly through the ISRAI Order of Preference.

Memory aid: I – S – R – A – I (Order of Preference for Filling Vacancies)
1. INSTITUTION Primary disposition in the will if the instituted heir can and does accept.
↓
2. SUBSTITUTION (Free Portion) Express will of the testator (Art. 859) always defeats the implied will of accretion.
↓
3. REPRESENTATION (Legitime / Intestacy) In compulsory or legal succession (predecease/incapacity/disinheritance), representation defeats accretion.
↓
4. ACCRETION (Art. 1016 / Art. 1018) Applies if no substitute or representative exists, provided Art. 1016 requisites concur (never on legitime).
↓
5. INTESTACY (Art. 1022) Fallback rule: passes to the testator's legal heirs subject to the same charges and obligations.

8. Devisees, Legatees, Usufructuaries & Selective Repudiation (Art. 1023)

Imagine an elderly aunt who leaves a life usufruct over her rental commercial building conjointly to her three nephews "for as long as they live." Ten years after her death, one nephew dies. Does his 1/3 share of the rental income revert to the naked owner, or go to the two surviving nephews?

Under Article 1023, accretion takes place among devisees, legatees, and usufructuaries under the same conditions established for heirs. In Policarpio v. Salamat (L-21809, Jan. 31, 1966), the Supreme Court held that when a life usufruct is constituted conjointly, the death of one co-usufructuary causes his share to accrue to the surviving co-usufructuaries—not revert to the naked owners—until the death of the last surviving usufructuary.

Symmetric Contrast: Selective Acceptance & Repudiation of Accretion

ACCEPT ORIGINAL + RENOUNCE ACCRETION = VALID  |  RENOUNCE ORIGINAL + ACCEPT ACCRETION = VOID

✓ Can an heir ACCEPT the original share and REPUDIATE the accretion?

YES (Majority View: Castan, Tolentino, Caguioa, Jurado, Paras). Accretion is an optional right granted by law (jus accrescendi), distinct from the original share derived from the express will. Thus, an heir may decline an accrued share burdened with heavy charges under Art. 1020.

✗ Can an heir REPUDIATE the original share and ACCEPT the accretion?

NO (Absolute Rule). The original institution is the indispensable legal foundation (root) for the accessory right of accretion. Without accepting the primary share, no right to accretion can ever come into existence.

Top 5 Rules to Prioritize for Examination

  1. No Accretion on the Legitime (Art. 1021): Always split a compulsory heir's vacant inheritance into two buckets—the Legitime passes to legal heirs in their own right (using statutory intestate ratios like 2:2:1), while the Free Portion passes by accretion (using testamentary proportions under Art. 1019).
  2. The ISRAI Hierarchy (Art. 1022): In the free portion, Substitution defeats Accretion. In the legitime or intestacy, Representation defeats Accretion. Only when both are absent does Accretion apply, followed last by Intestacy.
  3. Earmarked vs. Aliquot Shares (Art. 1017): Unequal fractional shares (1/4 and 3/4) allow accretion because they create co-ownership pro indiviso. Assigning specific physical parts ("Door 1 to A, Door 2 to B" or "BPI account to A, BDO account to B") bars accretion.
  4. Repudiation Never Allows Representation (Arts. 977 & 1018): If an heir repudiates, his children cannot represent him—his free-portion or intestate share goes to his co-heirs by accretion (unless all co-heirs in that degree repudiate, triggering Art. 969).
  5. One-Way Selective Acceptance: You may accept the original share and renounce the accretion, but you can never renounce the original share and accept the accretion.
