PART I — Intestate Succession, Concurrence of Heirs, and Mixed Succession

1. Fundamental Principles, Causes of Intestacy & Jurisprudence (Art. 960)

Imagine a wealthy businesswoman who never bothered to draft a will because she assumed the law would distribute her property fairly among her family. When she dies suddenly, her relatives discover her estate is divided entirely by statutory rules based on presumed natural affection.

Legal or intestate succession is the transmission of hereditary rights established by operation of law in default of a valid and operative testamentary disposition. It rests upon the presumed will of the decedent ("voluntas defuncti"), allocating the entire net hereditary estate in accordance with natural affection, proximity of blood, and familial duty.

Memory aid: N – P – F – P – R – E (Causes of Intestacy — Art. 960)
The six statutory and doctrinal grounds triggering legal succession under Article 960.

Key Core Principles in Pure Intestacy:
  • Absence of a Free Portion: In pure intestacy, there are no separate dispositive "legitime" and "free portion" buckets. The entire net estate (100%) is distributed among the legal heirs according to the statutory proportions.
  • Preservation of Compulsory Shares: When compulsory heirs concur in intestacy (e.g., legitimate children with illegitimate children), their statutory legitimes act as an untouchable baseline floor.
  • The State as Ultimate Default Heir (Arts. 1011–1014): Under Articles 1011–1014, the State stands at the end of both regular and irregular lines. An estate can never remain permanently ownerless.

2. Rules of Thumb and Specific Combinations in Intestate Shares

Imagine an estate lawyer tasked with advising the surviving family of a decedent who left a spouse, two legitimate parents, and three illegitimate children. Without a guiding formula, calculating their respective fractional entitlements feels like solving simultaneous quadratic equations.

To master intestate shares without error, apply the One-Half Rule of Thumb: Whenever the legitimate direct line (legitimate children/descendants, or in their default, legitimate parents/ascendants) concurs with irregular compulsory heirs, the legitimate line consistently receives at least one-half (1/2) of the net estate, and every fraction of the estate must be exhausted (100% total).

Status of Adopted Children in Intestacy:

Under Philippine adoption statutes (R.A. 8552 and R.A. 11642), an adopted child is deemed a legitimate child of the adopter for all civil and successional purposes. Consequently, when succeeding ab intestato from the adopter, the adopted child inherits the exact same share as a biological legitimate child, never the reduced share of an illegitimate child.

⚡ The Favorite Examination Trap (Whole vs. Half-Blood Collaterals):
If a decedent dies intestate leaving two full-blood first cousins and one half-blood first cousin, what are their shares? Common exam error: awarding the full-blood cousins double the share of the half-blood cousin. Correct Rule: Under Article 1009, first cousins succeed without distinction of lines or whole/half blood! The estate is divided into three equal parts (1/3 each). The 2:1 rule applies strictly to brothers, sisters, nephews, and nieces!

3. Concurrence of Legitimate and Illegitimate Children in Intestacy

Imagine a decedent who dies intestate leaving two legitimate children, two illegitimate children, and a net estate of ₱120 Million. How do you reconcile the statutory mandate of a 2:1 ratio with the constitutional and statutory protection guaranteeing the legitimate children's legitime?

Under Article 983 in relation to Article 895 (as amended by Article 176 of the Family Code), illegitimate children inherit with legitimate children in the proportion of two-to-one (2:1)—such that each illegitimate child receives one-half (1/2) of the share of each legitimate child—provided that the legitime of the legitimate children (1/2 of the net estate) is never impaired.

Mandatory 3-Step Procedure for Concurrence:
STEP 1: SATISFY LEGITIMES FIRST Allocate 1/2 of the estate to the legitimate children, divided equally among them.
↓
STEP 2: ALLOCATE ILLEGITIMATE CHILDREN'S SHARES Each illegitimate child gets 1/2 of the legitime of 1 legitimate child from the remaining half.
↓
STEP 3: RESOLVE THE SURPLUS (THE TWO THEORIES) If a surplus remains, apply either the Exclusion Theory or the Concurrence Theory.

4. Mixed Succession and the Rule on Charging Legacies

Imagine a wealthy man who executes a one-page will stating: "I leave ₱10 Million to my best friend Sarah," without disposing of the remaining ₱110 Million of his estate. How must the executor pay Sarah without violating the Civil Code?

Mixed succession occurs when a decedent dies leaving a valid will that disposes of only a part of his estate without instituting a universal heir (Art. 960, par. 2). Testamentary succession governs the legacy/devise, while intestate succession governs the undisposed balance.

The Fatal Error vs. The Lawful Rule:
  • The Fatal Top-Line Error: Deducting the ₱10M legacy directly off the gross/net estate at the start (reducing the base from ₱120M to ₱110M) and then distributing the reduced ₱110M. This is completely illegal because it shrinks the computational base and illegally impairs the legitimes of compulsory heirs!
  • The Lawful 3-Step Sequence:
    1. Compute both theoretical intestate shares and statutory legitimes on the entire gross/net estate (ignoring the legacy temporarily).
    2. Compare intestate share vs. legitime: find which legal heir has an intestate share that exceeds his statutory legitime.
    3. Deduct the legacy exclusively from that heir's excess intestate share!

Why Top-Line Deduction Fails:
If Sarah's ₱10M is deducted off the top, the remaining base is ₱110M. Under Art. 1000, Parents A & B would get 1/2 of ₱110M = ₱55M. But their statutory legitime is 1/2 of ₱120M = ₱60M! Thus, deducting the legacy off the top would illegally deprive the legitimate parents of ₱5 Million of their protected legitime!

PART II — Provisions Common to Testate and Intestate Succession: Right of Accretion & Resolution of Vacancies

5. Concept, Nature, and Requisites of Accretion (Arts. 1015–1017)

Imagine a testator who leaves his entire beach resort in San Fernando, La Union, to three childhood friends in equal shares without naming substitutes. When one friend renounces his share, what happens to that vacant one-third?

Under Article 1015 of the Civil Code, the right of accretion is that right by virtue of which, when two or more persons are called to the same inheritance, legacy, or devise, the part assigned to the one who renounces (repudiates), cannot receive (incapacitated), or dies before the testator (predeceased), is added or incorporated into that of his co-heirs, co-legatees, or co-devisees.

Memory aid: U – P – V – A (Requisites of Accretion — Arts. 1015–1017)
Four indispensable conditions for accretion to operate.
LetterRequisiteLegal Meaning & Statutory Application
UUnity of ObjectTwo or more persons must be called to the exact same inheritance, legacy, or devise (single mass or property).
PPlurality Pro IndivisoCalled without special designation of physical parts. Aliquot fractions ("one-half each") do not bar accretion (Art. 1017). Only physical earmarking bars accretion.
VVacancy (R-I-P)Share becomes vacant through Repudiation, Incapacity, or Predecease (or failed suspensive condition).

⚡ The Favorite Examination Trap (Aliquot Shares vs. Physical Earmarking):
Does the phrase "I devise my Sevilla farm to A and B, one-half each" bar accretion? NO! Under Article 1017, designating aliquot fractional parts does not exclude accretion. Accretion is barred only by physical earmarking (e.g., "the northern half to A, the southern half to B" or "Metrobank account to A, BPI account to B").

6. Accretion in Legal Succession vs. Testamentary Succession (Arts. 1018, 1021)

Imagine a law student reviewing Article 1018 and noticing it specifies only "the person who repudiates," omitting predecease and incapacity altogether. Did the codifiers make a drafting mistake?

Under Article 1018, in legal succession, the share of the person who repudiates shall always accrue to his co-heirs. Justice Desiderio Jurado explained that the omission of predecease and incapacity was deliberate and technically precise.

The Jurado Doctrine on Article 1018 vs. Analogous View:
Doctrinal ViewTheoretical Explanation of Article 1018
Justice Jurado's View
(Technically Precise)		 In legal succession, when an heir predeceases or is incapacitated, either he is represented by his descendants under Art. 970, or (if no descendants) he never acquired hereditary rights at death—so surviving co-heirs inherit the entire estate in their own right (iure proprio) from the outset. Only a repudiating heir actually acquired a vested call to the succession and cannot be represented (Art. 977), creating a true post-opening vacancy passing by accretion!
Broad / Analogous View If a predeceased or incapacitated intestate heir leaves no representative, the practical result is identical to accretion, as the surviving co-heirs absorb the expanded balance.
Accretion Among Compulsory Heirs (Article 1021):

Under Article 1021, among compulsory heirs, accretion takes place only when the free portion is left to two or more of them, or to any of them and a stranger.

The Bedrock Rule on Repudiated Legitimes (Art. 1021, par. 2):
"Should the part repudiated be the legitime, the other co-heirs shall succeed to it in their own right, and not by the right of accretion."
Rationale: The legitime is conferred directly by operation of law upon a class of compulsory heirs. It does not emanate from the creative will of the testator. When one compulsory heir repudiates, his remaining co-heirs in the same class absorb the legitime in their own right (iure proprio).

  • Legitime (₱60M ÷ 4): ₱15 Million per child.

PART III — Capacity to Succeed by Will or Intestacy, Relative Incapacities, and Special Dispositions

9. General Capacity to Succeed, Conceived Children & Survivorship (Arts. 40–43, 1025)

Imagine a pregnant widow whose husband dies in a sudden industrial accident. Three days later, she goes into premature labor. Does the newborn child inherit the father's multimillion-peso estate, or do the deceased husband's brothers take everything?

Under Article 1025 of the Civil Code, to be capacitated to inherit, the heir, devisee, or legatee must be living at the moment the succession opens (the moment of death under Art. 777), except in case of representation when proper. A conceived child is capable of succeeding provided it acquires legal personality under Articles 40 and 41.

Crucial Bar Terminology: Intrauterine Life Rules (Articles 40 & 41)

Never write "when the baby is under seven months old." A seven-month-old infant is already living outside the womb! Always use the exact statutory phrase: intrauterine life.

Rule 1: Intrauterine Life < 7 Months

If the fetus had an intrauterine life of less than seven months, it is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four (24) hours after complete delivery from the maternal womb (cutting of umbilical cord + 24 hrs survival required).

Rule 2: Intrauterine Life ≥ 7 Months

If the fetus had an intrauterine life of at least seven months, it acquires full civil personality the moment it is completely delivered alive from the maternal womb. If it dies 5 minutes after birth, it succeeded to the estate and transmits that share to its heirs!

Presumption of Simultaneous Death & Representation (Article 43):

Under Article 43, if two or more persons called to succeed each other perish in a common disaster and there is no proof of who died first, they are presumed to have died at the same time and there is no transmission of rights between them.

Article 43 Does NOT Bar Representation!
While no hereditary rights transmit from father X to son A if they die simultaneously in a car crash, A's children (grandchildren B & C) are NOT barred from inheriting A's share! Under Article 970, representation does not transmit rights through the deceased son; the grandchildren step directly into the son's shoes to inherit directly from the grandfather X!
Visual Scaffolding: Simultaneous Death (Art. 43) vs. Representation (Art. 970)
Article 43 prevents transmission from X to A, but grandchildren inherit directly from X via representation.

10. Incapacity by Reason of Possible Undue Influence (Art. 1027)

Imagine a dying patient in a hospital room who confesses his sins to a visiting priest, receives medical treatments from his attending physician, and then executes a holographic will leaving the free portion of his estate to the priest, the doctor, and his guardian. Are these bequests valid?

Article 1027 of the Civil Code enumerates six classes of persons disqualified from testamentary dispositions due to a conclusive presumption of undue influence during extreme vulnerability, spiritual dependence, or fiduciary authority.

Memory aid: P – R – G – W – P – E (The 6 Disqualifications — Art. 1027)
Disqualifies beneficiaries from testamentary dispositions (free portion).
ClassDisqualified PersonClassroom Nuances & Key Exceptions
1. Priest Priest hearing confession / minister extending spiritual aid during last illness. Sequential Confessions: If testator confessed to 5 priests on the same day, all 5 are disqualified! Praying the Rosary may qualify as spiritual aid. Exception: Will made while healthy before last illness is valid.
2. Relatives Relatives of such priest within 4th civil degree and his church/order. Prevents circumvention. Extends to cousins, nephews, aunts, and brothers of the priest, and his religious congregation.
3. Guardian Guardian before approval of final accounts of the guardianship. Express Statutory Exception: Disqualification does not apply if the guardian is an ascendant, descendant, brother, sister, or spouse of the ward!
4. Witness Attesting witness to the will, spouse, parents, or children (Art. 823). Exception: Valid if there are more than three (3) other credible, competent witnesses.
5. Medical Physician, surgeon, nurse, health officer, or druggist (pharmacist) caring during last illness. Must have actually taken care of patient. A consulting specialist is NOT disqualified! Child-doctor debate: Prof submits filial doctor-child should inherit free portion.
6. Entities Associations, corporations, or entities prohibited by law. Entities lacking legal personality or prohibited by Constitution/statutes from owning land/inheriting.
⚡ The Favorite Examination Trap (Article 1027 Scope):
Article 1027 applies exclusively to testamentary dispositions (free portion)! It NEVER impairs legitimes and NEVER applies to intestate succession! If a priest or doctor is a legitimate child of the testator, he still receives his full statutory legitime by mandate of law.
Professor's ₱120M Classroom Illustration (Art. 1027 Nuances):

Testator X dies leaving a ₱120 Million estate, survived by his first cousin (FC) and three legitimate children: Father A (priest who heard X's confession during last illness), Dr. B (physician who cared for X during last illness), and C (ordinary child). X's will leaves everything to A, B, and C.

  • Legitime (₱60M): A, B, and C first receive their ₱20M legitime each. Article 1027 never impairs the legitime!
  • Free Portion (₱60M):
    • Professor's Preferred View (Filial Exception): A, B, and C take ₱20M each of the free portion because the law should not penalize children for caring for a dying parent.
    • Strict Textual View: Father A is disqualified under par. 1; Dr. B is disqualified under par. 5; and C is disqualified under par. 2 as a 2nd-degree sibling of Father A! The ₱60M free portion passes by intestacy. Even if the children were excluded in intestacy, First Cousin FC (4th degree from X, but 5th degree from Father A) lies safely outside the 4th-degree prohibition of par. 2!

11. Incapacity Arising from Morality, Dispositions for the Soul & Adoption Rules

Imagine a testator who leaves ₱50 Million "for prayers for the benefit of my soul," and in another clause devises a townhouse to his illicit paramour. Does the paramour inherit, and does the State take half of the prayer fund?

Three Special Dispositive Rules:
1. ADULTERY / CONCUBINAGE (ART. 1028 IN REL. TO ART. 739) Dispositions between persons guilty of adultery or concubinage at the time of the will are void for public policy. Paramours/mistresses cannot inherit under the will.
↓
2. DISPOSITIONS FOR PRAYERS & PIOUS WORKS (ART. 1029) If made in general terms without specifying application: 50% to Testator's Church and 50% to the State (Art. 1013 public purposes).
Key Exception: If the testator specifies the application, churches, or masses (e.g., 1,000 masses in Baclaran/Quiapo), Art. 1029 does not apply and 100% goes to the specified application!
↓
3. SUCCESSION IN ADOPTION (R.A. 11642 & ART. 972) Adopted child inherits directly from adopter as a legitimate child. Absolute Bar: An adopted child can neither represent nor be represented in succession to/from the adopter's relatives! Filiation in adoption is strictly personal between adopter and adoptee.
⚡ The Favorite Examination Trap (Representation in Adoption):
If grandfather Y dies intestate and his son X (who adopted child A) predeceased Y, does A represent X in Y's estate? NO! An adopted child cannot represent the adopter in the estate of the adopter's parents or collaterals. A gets nothing from grandfather Y!
