Capacity to Succeed by Will or by Intestacy

Civil Code Articles 1024–1040 (in relation to Articles 40–41, 739, 823, 845, 922, and 1013)

Our approach: Understand the situation first, identify the applicable rule, memorize the requisites, and practice applying the law.

⚠️ Important Legal Update (R.A. No. 11642 on Adopted Children & Representation under Art. 1035):
In traditional classroom discussions of Article 1035, lectures cite Sayson v. Court of Appeals (G.R. No. 89224, 1992), which held that an adopted child of an excluded/predeceased heir cannot represent the adopter in the succession to the adopter's parents because adoption created a personal relationship solely between adopter and adoptee. However, under Section 41 of R.A. No. 11642 (Domestic Administrative Adoption and Alternative Child Care Act, effective January 2022), the legitimate filiation created by adoption now extends to the adopter's parents, legitimate siblings, and legitimate descendants for all intents and purposes, including succession. For exams: Always state the classic Sayson v. CA doctrine emphasized in your lecture notes, and then cite Sec. 41 of R.A. 11642 as the modern statutory rule.

PART I — GENERAL PRINCIPLES & NATURAL/JURIDICAL CAPACITY (ARTS. 1024–1026)

1. Passive Testamentary Capacity & Scope of Incapacity (Article 1024)

Imagine... A wealthy grandfather dies leaving a mansion to his 2-year-old grandson and a commercial building to a diagnosed schizophrenic nephew. Angry relatives claim neither can inherit because neither can sign contracts or manage property.

The Rule: Capacity to succeed (passive testamentary capacity) is the legal ability to receive and retain property mortis causa; under Article 1024, all natural and juridical persons not specially incapacitated by law may succeed, because succession requires only juridical capacity (civil personality), never capacity to act.

Memory aid: MAKING A WILL = CAPACITY TO ACT; RECEIVING AN INHERITANCE = JURIDICAL CAPACITY ONLY
ABSOLUTE INCAPACITY

Cannot inherit from anyone under any circumstance:
1. Not living or conceived at decedent's death (Art. 1025)
2. Entities lacking personality (except Art. 1026/1029/1030)
3. Unidentifiable / unknown heirs (Art. 845)

RELATIVE INCAPACITY

Disqualified only regarding specific persons or properties:
1. Possible Undue Influence (Art. 1027, Nos. 1–5)
2. Public Policy & Morality (Art. 1028 / Art. 739)
3. Unworthiness (Art. 1032)

🪤 The Favorite Examination Trap (Article 1024, Par. 2):
Article 1024(2) states that provisions on incapacity by will apply equally to intestate succession—but this is NOT absolute! Disqualifications under Article 1027 (Nos. 1–5) and Article 1028 apply exclusively to testamentary succession (and never bar intestate shares or compulsory legitimes). Only Unworthiness (Art. 1032) and Absolute Incapacity apply to both testate and intestate succession.

2. Natural Persons & the Conceived Child (Article 1025)

Imagine... A man executes a will leaving ₱1 Million to his best friend, who had actually died the week before, and another ₱1 Million to his pregnant sister's unborn baby. Three months after the testator dies, the baby is born prematurely at 6 months of gestation and dies 12 hours after delivery.

The Rule: Under Article 1025, a natural person must be living or at least conceived at the exact moment the succession opens (decedent's death under Art. 777); a disposition to an already-deceased person is void ab initio (Resurreccion v. Javier), while a conceived child (nasciturus) inherits only if subsequently born alive in compliance with Articles 40 and 41.

Memory aid: 7 MONTHS = ALIVE AT BIRTH; < 7 MONTHS = 24-HOUR SURVIVAL
Intra-Uterine Life (Art. 41) Survival Requirement After Complete Delivery Legal Effect if Child Dies 10 Hours After Birth
7 months or more Must simply be born alive (even for an instant). Acquired personality & inherited. Its share passes to its own heirs.
Less than 7 months Must remain alive for at least 24 hours after complete separation from womb. Never deemed born. Acquires zero successional rights.
Prof. Paras & Dr. Tolentino Critique on Art. 1025's "except in case of representation" clause: The phrasing is grammatically defective. The representative must still be living or conceived when succession opens; it is the person represented who need not be alive.

3. Juridical Persons & Unincorporated Associations (Article 1026)

Imagine... A philanthropist leaves a legacy in her will to a neighborhood soup kitchen that has operated for ten years as a charitable association for poor children, even though it never registered with the SEC.

The Rule: Under Article 1026, the State, provinces, municipal corporations, and validly organized private corporations may inherit by will, and by express statutory exception, even non-incorporated organizations or associations organized for religious, scientific, cultural, educational, or charitable purposes may receive testamentary dispositions.

Memory aid: C-R-E-S-C (Unincorporated Associations Allowed by Will)
Five purposes that empower even an unincorporated association to inherit by will under Article 1026.
CCulturalAssociations promoting arts, heritage, or culture.
RReligiousReligious fellowships or missions (subject to constitutional land limits).
EEducationalStudy groups, scholarship societies, or educational bodies.
SScientificResearch or scientific associations.
CCharitableBenevolent or relief organizations.
Symmetric Contrast: TESTATE = State + Corporations + CRESC Associations | INTESTATE = ONLY THE STATE (never private corporations).

PART II — UNDUE INFLUENCE, PUBLIC MORALITY & SPECIAL INSTITUTIONS (ARTS. 1027–1031)

4. Relative Incapacities Based on Possible Undue Influence (Article 1027)

Imagine... On his deathbed during his final illness, an elderly patient confesses his sins to a parish priest and is cared for daily by his attending physician. Grateful and vulnerable, he executes a will leaving half his free portion to the priest's religious order and the other half to his doctor.

The Rule: Article 1027 establishes a conclusive presumption of undue influence or self-interest—meaning no proof of actual undue influence is required and no evidence is admissible to disprove it—disqualifying five specific classes of persons from receiving voluntary testamentary dispositions.

Memory aid: P – R – G – W – M – L (The 6 Paragraphs of Article 1027)
Memorize the exact scope, timing requisite, and statutory escape hatch for each paragraph of Article 1027.
Class (Art. 1027) Strict Requisites for Disqualification When Valid / Exception
1 & 2. Priest / Minister & 4th-Degree Relatives / Church (a) Heard confession or extended spiritual aid; (b) during last illness; (c) will executed during or after said confession/aid. Extends to priest's blood relatives within 4th degree & his church/order. Valid if will was made before the confession/aid, or long after recovery, or involves intestate share / legitime.
3. Guardian Disposition made by ward before final accounts of guardianship are judicially approved (even if testator dies after approval). Valid if guardian is an Ascendant, Descendant, Brother, Sister, or Spouse (A-D-B-S-S), or will is made after approval.
4. Attesting Witness Attesting witness, or their spouse, parents, or children. (Note: Under 2004 Notarial Rules, a Notary Public cannot authenticate a will if he or his spouse benefits). Valid under Art. 823 if there are 3 other competent and disinterested witnesses to the will.
5. Medical Caregivers Physicians, surgeons, nurses, health officers, or druggists who took regular/continuous care of testator during his last illness. Isolated emergency care does not disqualify. (J.B.L. Reyes critique: should exempt close relatives acting out of filial piety).
6. Entities Prohibited by Law Individuals, associations, and corporations not permitted by law to inherit. Reg. of Deeds v. Ung Siu Si Temple: Unregistered alien Chinese temple cannot acquire Philippine land under the Constitution.
Landmark Case Visual: Torres v. Lopez (49 Phil. 504) — Guardian vs. Guardian's Daughter
Unlike priests (where 4th-degree relatives are barred) and witnesses (where spouse/parents/children are barred), Article 1027(3) does NOT extend to the guardian's relatives. Thus, Luz Lopez inherited both her own share and her father's void share via accretion!

5. Incapacity by Reason of Public Morality (Article 1028 in rel. to Article 739)

Imagine... A married man writes in his will: "Although I am legally married to Rufina, I leave my entire free portion to Sofia, with whom I have been living as my partner for twenty years."

The Rule: Under Article 1028, the prohibitions against donations inter vivos in Article 739 apply equally to testamentary dispositions, rendering void ab initio any bequest triggered by adultery/concubinage, mutual criminality, or public office.

Memory aid: A – C – P (Three Void Dispositions under Art. 739 / 1028)
AAdultery / ConcubinageMade between persons guilty of adultery or concubinage at the time of execution. No criminal conviction needed; preponderance of evidence in civil/probate court suffices.
CCriminal OffenseMade between persons found guilty of the same criminal offense, in consideration thereof (e.g., murder pact).
PPublic OfficerMade to a public officer or his spouse, ascendants, or descendants, by reason of his office.
Landmark Ruling — Nepomuceno v. CA (139 SCRA 206): While probate courts generally pass only upon extrinsic validity, where the intrinsic nullity is patent on the face of the will (testator admitted concubinage in the will itself), the probate court may immediately declare the devise to the concubine void ab initio.

6. Institution of the Soul (Art. 1029) & the Poor in General (Art. 1030)

Imagine... A devout widow leaves a tract of land "for prayers and pious works for the benefit of my soul" without naming any church, and leaves ₱2 Million "to the poor" without naming any charity or city.

The Rule: As statutory exceptions to the rule that heirs must be identified persons with juridical personality, Article 1029 splits general dispositions for the soul 50–50 between the testator's church and the State, while Article 1030 directs dispositions for the "poor in general" to the poor domiciled in the testator's domicile at death.

When Testator Disposes for Prayers/Pious Works in General Terms (No Specific Application):
The executor sells the property (with court approval) and distributes the proceeds 50–50:

The State (Art. 1013)
Exception (Villavicencio v. Quinio, 67 Phil. 367): If the testator specifies the application, specific masses, or particular churches/relatives, Article 1029 does NOT apply; the testator's express instructions govern 100%.

Locality Rule: Limited to the poor living in the domicile of the testator at the time of his death.

7. Interposition of Dummies & Intermediaries (Article 1031)

Imagine... Knowing he cannot leave land directly to his mistress under Article 1028, a testator writes in his will that he "owes ₱5 Million" to the mistress's brother (who never lent him a centavo) and directs his executor to pay that simulated debt out of the estate.

The Rule: Under Article 1031, any testamentary disposition in favor of a disqualified person—even if disguised as an onerous contract / simulated credit or made through an intermediary (dummy)—is void ab initio (quod contra legem fit, nihil fit).

🪤 The Favorite Examination Trap (Art. 1031 Applies to 1027 & 1028, NOT to 1032!):
Why does Article 1031's anti-dummy rule apply only to Undue Influence (Art. 1027) and Public Morality (Art. 1028), and not to Unworthiness (Art. 1032)? Because if a testator already knows of an heir's act of unworthiness under Art. 1032 and still chooses to benefit him in a will (directly or indirectly), the law treats that disposition as an Implied Pardon under Article 1033!

PART III — INCAPACITY BY REASON OF UNWORTHINESS & PARDON (ARTS. 1032–1033)

8. The Eight Statutory Grounds for Unworthiness (Article 1032)

Imagine... A son tries to poison his father and is convicted of frustrated parricide. The father dies intestate without leaving a will disinheriting the son. Can the son still claim his intestate share and legitime because there was no will?

The Rule: No. Under Article 1032, incapacity by reason of unworthiness operates by force of law in both testate and intestate succession, automatically stripping the offender of both the free portion and the compulsory legitime across 8 enumerated grounds.

Memory aid: A – L – F – V – A – C – P – F (The 8 Grounds of Art. 1032 Clustered into 3 Buckets)
Grouped into 3 thematic buckets for rapid Bar recall, with critical doctrinal qualifiers.
Thematic Bucket Grounds under Article 1032 Critical Doctrinal Nuances
1. Family & Marital Integrity
(Nos. 1 & 5)		 (1) Parents who abandon their children, induce daughters to lead a corrupt/immoral life, or attempt against their virtue.
(5) Person convicted of adultery or concubinage with the spouse of the testator.		 Notice: Under Art. 1032(5), the paramour/lover is unworthy after final conviction. (By contrast, under Art. 1028/739, no conviction is required to void a testamentary bequest between paramours).
2. Life, Liberty & Violent Death
(Nos. 2, 3 & 4)		 (2) Convicted of an attempt against the life of the testator, his/her spouse, descendants, or ascendants.
(3) Accused testator of a crime carrying 6+ years imprisonment (prision correccional max or higher), if the accusation is found groundless.
(4) Heir of full age (18+) who fails to report testator's violent death to authorities within 1 month.		 • No. 2: Requires final conviction and intent to kill (dolo, never culpa). Executive pardon does NOT erase unworthiness, unless granted due to proven innocence or amnesty.
• No. 3 (Javier v. Lucero): Acquittal based merely on reasonable doubt does NOT make the accusation "groundless."
• No. 4 (J.B.L. Reyes): Practically inoperative today since crimes are prosecuted de oficio and citizens have no legal obligation to file a formal accusation.
3. Testamentary Freedom & Wills
(Nos. 6, 7 & 8)		 (6) Causes testator to make or change a will by fraud, violence, intimidation, or undue influence.
(7) Prevents decedent from making/revoking a will, or supplants, conceals, or alters the latter's will.
(8) Falsifies or forges a supposed will of the decedent.		 No criminal conviction is required for Nos. 6, 7, and 8; civil proof in the estate proceeding suffices.

9. Pardon & Condonation of Unworthiness vs. Reconciliation (Art. 1033 vs. Art. 922)

Imagine... After a daughter is convicted of attempting to kill her mother, the mother visits her in prison, hugs her, and verbally forgives her in front of the prison chaplain, but never signs any written document or new will before dying.

The Rule: Under Article 1033, causes of unworthiness are erased only inside a strict two-track system: Implied Pardon (if the testator already knew of the offense when executing a will instituting the offender) or Express Written Pardon (if the testator learned of it later, or died intestate, requiring a written public or private document).

Memory aid: KNEW BEFORE WILL = IMPLIED BY INSTITUTION; LEARNED AFTER / NO WILL = MUST BE IN WRITING
Feature Implied Pardon (Art. 1033) Express Pardon (Art. 1033) Reconciliation in Disinheritance (Art. 922)
Situation Testator already knew of the unworthiness when he executed the will. Testator learned of it after making the will, or made no will. Testator expressly disinherited the compulsory heir in a valid will.
How Effected By instituting the offender or leaving him a bequest in the will. Must be condoned in writing (public or private document). Oral pardon is void! Mutual reconciliation (bilateral resumption of family relations); need NOT be in writing.
Effect of Revocation If the will containing the institution is revoked, the implied pardon is also revoked. Irrevocable once executed in writing. Renders the express disinheritance ineffectual (prevailing view: Balane, Tolentino, Paras).

PART IV — DETERMINATION, REPRESENTATION & REMEDIES (ARTS. 1034–1040)

10. Time to Judge Capacity & Suspensive Conditions (Article 1034)

Imagine... A testator leaves a condo to his nephew "if he passes the Bar exam." The nephew is alive when the testator dies in 2024, but tragically dies in a car crash in 2025 before the Bar results come out.

The Rule: Under Article 1034, capacity to succeed is generally judged at the exact moment of the decedent's death; however, if the institution is subject to a suspensive condition, the heir must be capacitated BOTH at the time of the decedent's death AND at the time the condition is fulfilled.

Memory aid: PURE = AT DEATH; FINAL JUDGMENT = RETROACTS TO DEATH; SUSPENSIVE CONDITION = DEATH + FULFILLMENT
1. GENERAL RULE (Art. 1034, Par. 1) Judged at the exact moment of the decedent's death (Art. 777).
↓
2. GROUNDS REQUIRING FINAL CONVICTION / JUDGMENT (Art. 1032 Nos. 2, 3, 5) Wait for the final judgment to be rendered; once final, incapacity retroacts to the moment of death.
↓
3. SUSPENSIVE CONDITIONAL INSTITUTION (Art. 1034, Par. 3) Heir must have capacity at TWO moments: (a) Decedent's death AND (b) Fulfillment of the condition. (If heir dies before fulfillment, he transmits zero rights to his own heirs).

11. Right of Representation for Unworthy Compulsory Heirs (Article 1035)

Imagine... A father with a ₱600,000 estate leaves a will dividing his estate equally among his three children, A, B, and C (₱200,000 each). Child A is convicted of attempting to kill the father, but A has two innocent children of his own, X and Y.

The Rule: Under Article 1035, if an unworthy compulsory heir is excluded, his children or descendants acquire his right to the legitime by representation (while in testate succession, his voluntary share in the free portion passes to co-heirs by accretion), and the unworthy parent is strictly barred from enjoying the usufruct or administration of the property inherited by his children.

Family Tree & Share Split: Representation of Unworthy Heir A (₱600,000 Estate)
Key Distinction: In testate succession, X and Y represent A only as to A's ₱100,000 legitime (₱50k each), while A's ₱100,000 free portion share accrues to B and C (₱50k each). In complete intestacy, X and Y would represent A's entire ₱200,000 intestate share (₱100k each)!

12. Alienations, Expenses, Bad Faith, Conflict of Laws & Prescription (Arts. 1036–1040)

Imagine... An unworthy heir takes possession of an inherited farm right after the decedent's death and sells a tractor to an innocent buyer before the court issues a judicial order of exclusion. Six years later, the other heirs sue to declare him incapacitated and recover the farm.

The Rule: Articles 1036 to 1040 govern the post-death consequences of incapacity: protecting innocent third-party buyers before judicial exclusion (Art. 1036), reimbursing necessary preservation expenses (Art. 1037), penalizing the incapacitated possessor as a conclusive possessor in bad faith (Art. 1038), applying the decedent's national law (Art. 1039), and fixing a 5-year prescriptive period from physical possession (Art. 1040).

Provision Core Legal Rule Key Exam Qualification / Remedy
Article 1036
Alienations Before Exclusion		 Alienations & acts of administration by the excluded heir before the judicial order of exclusion are VALID as to third persons in good faith. Co-heirs cannot recover the property from the innocent buyer; their sole remedy is to claim damages from the disqualified heir.
Article 1037
Expenses & Credits		 Excluded heir may demand: (1) reimbursement for necessary expenses to preserve the property, and (2) payment of personal credits/debts against the estate. Even a possessor in bad faith is entitled to necessary preservation expenses (Art. 546) and does not forfeit pre-existing valid credits.
Article 1038
Possessor in Bad Faith		 Incapacitated person who takes possession in spite of prohibition is conclusively presumed a possessor in bad faith. Must return the property + all accessions, fruits, and rents actually received or which could have been received through due diligence.
Article 1039
Conflict of Laws		 Capacity to succeed is governed by the national law of the decedent (reiterating Art. 16, Par. 2). If an Australian dies leaving Philippine land to a Filipino, Australian law governs the Filipino heir's capacity to succeed.
Article 1040
5-Year Prescription		 Action for declaration of incapacity and recovery of inheritance must be brought within 5 years from the time the disqualified person took possession. Applies to Art. 1027 & Art. 1032. Does NOT apply to Art. 1028 (Art. 739 immorality), which is void ab initio and imprescriptible!

Top 5 Rules to Prioritize for Examination

  1. Scope of Disqualifications (Testate Only vs. Both): Articles 1027(1–5) and 1028 invalidate only voluntary testamentary dispositions and never impair compulsory legitimes or intestate shares; Article 1032 (Unworthiness) operates in both testate and intestate succession and forfeits even the legitime.
  2. Whose Relatives Are Barred under Art. 1027? For a Priest, blood relatives within the 4th degree are barred; for an Attesting Witness, the spouse, parents, and children are barred; for a Guardian (Torres v. Lopez) and Physician/Nurse, their relatives are NOT barred (unless used as a dummy under Art. 1031).
  3. Written Pardon (Art. 1033) vs. Reconciliation (Art. 922): If the testator did not expressly disinherit the unworthy heir in a will, oral forgiveness or reconciliation is useless—Article 1033 requires a written condonation (or subsequent institution in a will with knowledge).
  4. Representation of an Unworthy Compulsory Heir in Testate Succession (Art. 1035): The unworthy heir's children represent him only as to his compulsory legitime (with the parent stripped of usufruct and administration), while his voluntary share in the free portion passes to co-heirs by right of accretion.
  5. 5-Year Prescription Exception (Art. 1040): The 5-year prescriptive period from physical possession applies to Arts. 1027 and 1032, but never to Article 1028 (Art. 739 immorality), which is void ab initio and imprescriptible.
