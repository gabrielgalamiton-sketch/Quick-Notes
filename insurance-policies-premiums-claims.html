  • Electronic Policies: Under R.A. 8792 and Insurance Commission Circular No. 2014-47, insurance policies, certificates, and endorsements may now be lawfully issued in electronic format with full legal effect, authenticity, and admissibility.

PART I — NATURE, FORM, CLASSIFICATIONS & ATTACHED DOCUMENTS (Secs. 49–66)

1. Policy of Insurance vs. Underlying Contract & Regulatory Requisites

Imagine an entrepreneur who signs an application for fire insurance covering his warehouse and leaves the branch office with the underwriting manager's oral approval. That night, an accidental electrical surge ignites the facility before any paper policy has been printed or delivered.

Under Section 49 of the Insurance Code (R.A. No. 10607), a policy of insurance is the written instrument in which a contract of insurance is set forth. While the underlying contract is consensual and perfected upon the meeting of the minds on subject, risk, and premium, the policy serves as the indispensable evidentiary embodiment required for enforcement before judicial and administrative tribunals.

Memory aid: CONSENT vs. PAPER Doctrinal Distinction
Contract of Insurance Policy of Insurance (Sec. 49)
Consensual contract perfected by mere mutual consent upon the essential elements: insurable interest, peril, and premium. The formal instrument (physical paper or electronic record) setting forth the terms, conditions, warranties, and exclusions.
Valid and binding between parties even if oral, provided all essential requisites concur. Must be submitted to and expressly approved by the Insurance Commissioner before issuance to the public.
Physical delivery is not indispensable to substantive creation unless stipulated as a condition precedent. Serves as conclusive external evidence of the terms agreed upon; signed by insurer, but insured's signature is not essential.

⚠️ The Favorite Examination Trap: Section 25 Statutory Prohibitions
Under Section 25 of the Insurance Code, any stipulation providing for the payment of loss regardless of whether the insured has an insurable interest, or declaring that the policy shall be conclusive proof of insurable interest, is VOID AB INITIO. Parties cannot contract away the requirement of insurable interest; doing so degenerates the insurance into an illegal wagering contract.

2. Classification of Policies: Open, Valued & Running (Secs. 60–62)

Imagine a gallery owner insuring an irreplaceable 19th-century oil painting, a commercial landlord insuring an office building, and a logistics titan storing fluctuating grain in a centralized silo. Should all three policies calculate indemnification in the exact same manner when disaster strikes?

The Insurance Code divides property policies into three distinct operational classifications based on how the subject matter is valued and how loss is measured at the moment of casualty.

Interactive Policy Valuation Calculator & Comparison Secs. 60–62

Open Policy (Sec. 60): The value of the property is not agreed upon in advance. The face amount represents solely the insurer's maximum liability ceiling. Recovery is pegged strictly to the proven sound value of the loss at the time of casualty.

Professor's Classroom Illustration: A commercial building is insured under an open policy for ₱5,000,000. It is totally destroyed by fire. Post-loss adjusters prove the sound market value at the time of the fire was ₱3,500,000. The insurer is liable only for ₱3,500,000. (Likewise, a car insured for ₱1,000,000 suffering ₱400,000 repairable collision damage pays ₱400,000, not ₱1,000,000).

3. Documents Attached: Warranties, Riders, Endorsements & Cover Notes (Sec. 52)

Imagine an industrial corporation whose printed fire policy contains a general exclusion for chemical fires, but stapled to the first page is a signed, typewritten rider extending coverage to chemical spills in exchange for an extra ₱50,000 premium. Which clause governs when a chemical fire occurs?

Under insurance jurisprudence, riders, endorsements, and specific clauses prevail over standard printed forms because they represent the specialized, proximate intention negotiated by the parties.

Comparative Matrix of Collateral Policy Documents
Document Type Legal Nature & Purpose Key Doctrinal Rule
Warranty Affirmation of fact or promise to maintain a condition (e.g. working fire extinguishers). Strict compliance required. Material breach avoids policy regardless of whether breach caused the loss.
Rider Slip attached to primary policy enlarging or restricting standard coverage. Prevails over printed body. Requires consideration (adjusted premium).
Endorsement Subsequent written modification (change of beneficiary, location, or coverage limit). Becomes an integral part of the contract from its stipulated effective date.
Cover Note (Sec. 52) Temporary interim contract pending formal underwriting review. Max 60 days. No separate premium permitted. Full policy liability attaches during its life.
Professor's Illustration on Section 52 Cover Notes: An entrepreneur applies for fire insurance and remits the deposit. The general agent issues an official cover note. On the 40th day, before the formal paper policy is printed or delivered, an electrical fire destroys the warehouse. The insurer remains fully liable! The cover note constitutes a binding interim contract of insurance.
⚠️ Examination Trap on Cover Notes: Three strict rules frequently tested in the Bar:
  1. Valid for a maximum of 60 days only (Sec. 52).
  2. May be extended only with the written approval of the Insurance Commissioner.
  3. No separate premium can be charged; the regular policy premium covers the interim protection.

4. Non-Life Cancellation & 45-Day Renewal Rules (Secs. 64–66)

Imagine a factory owner holding an annual property casualty policy. Eight months into the term, the insurer mails a postcard stating: "Your policy is cancelled effective immediately due to recent fires in your city." Two days later, a fire strikes. Is the cancellation valid?

Under Section 64 of the Insurance Code, an insurer cannot unilaterally cancel a policy of insurance other than life during its term unless founded exclusively upon subsequent statutory grounds and communicated via prior written notice.

PART II — PREMIUMS, CASH-AND-CARRY, ANTI-FORFEITURE & MULTIPLE INSURANCES (Secs. 77–100)

5. Concept, Consideration & Payment Modes of Premium (Secs. 77, 84)

Imagine a breadwinner who applies for a Variable Universal Life (VUL) policy. He remits his scheduled premium and deposits an extra ₱100,000 "top-up" payment via a post-dated check dated October 1. He dies on October 2, before the insurer encashes the check. Is the coverage active?

A premium is the agreed price or consideration (causa) paid by the insured for assuming the risk of indemnifying against casualty. Under Section 84, insurers may lawfully accept payments for future premiums.

VUL Dual Structural Framework & Payment Modes
1. Pure Mortality & Admin Charge

Funds the actuarial cost of pure death benefit protection and company overhead. Decreases as the policyholder ages.

2. Investment / Fund Value Component

Channeled into equity/bond funds. "Top-ups" increase investment value and cash accumulation, not the base death benefit face value.

Negotiable Instruments & Post-Dated Checks: Under general commercial law, a check produces payment only when cashed. However, if an insurer accepts a post-dated check bearing a date prior to the loss, and the drawer had sufficient funds in the bank on that date, payment is legally complete even if the insurer delayed depositing the check!

6. The Cash-and-Carry Rule & Its 5 Exclusive Exceptions (Sec. 77)

Imagine an insurer delivering a comprehensive fire policy stating: "Valid for 1 year; premium payable within 60 days." Thirty days later, a fire strikes before the premium is remitted. Can the insured enforce the policy?

Under Section 77 of the Insurance Code (the Cash-and-Carry Rule): "Notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary, no policy or contract of insurance is valid and binding until and unless the premium thereof has been paid..." This doctrine shields actuarial solvency.

Reconciliation: Consensual Perfection vs. Binding Consummation Sec. 77 Interplay
PERFECTION (Meeting of the Minds) Consensual contract is born when offer (application) meets unqualified acceptance (approval).
↓
STATUTORY CONDITION PRECEDENT (Sec. 77) Until premium is paid, the perfected contract remains dormant, inchoate, and UNENFORCEABLE against insurer!
Memory aid: G – A – I – C – E
The 5 exclusive statutory and jurisprudential exceptions to the Cash-and-Carry Rule.
G Grace Period In life and industrial life policies, 30 or 31-day grace period for subsequent premiums (Sec. 77). Death during grace period is covered.
A Acknowledgment Section 79: Written acknowledgment in the policy of receipt of premium is conclusive evidence of payment to make policy binding.
I Installments *Makati Tuscany v. CA* (1992): Acceptance of an initial installment makes policy valid and binding for the full term. Default doesn't void policy.
C Credit Term *UCPB v. Masagana Telamart* (2001 En Banc): Established practice of extending 60–90 day credit binds insurer if loss occurs within that window.
E Estoppel Insurer's representations, silence, or misleading conduct induces insured to believe coverage took effect.
⚠️ Examination Trap on Section 79 Acknowledgment:
The conclusive presumption of payment under Section 79 operates solely to make the policy binding and enforceable for loss claims. It does not bar the insurer from filing an ordinary civil action against the insured to collect the unpaid premium!

7. Return of Premiums & Life Anti-Forfeiture Devices (Secs. 80–84)

Imagine a policyholder who pays 3 years of premiums on a 20-year whole life policy and then loses his job, unable to pay further. Does the insurer simply confiscate every single peso paid?

Unlike property insurance where unearned premiums are returned pro-rata upon surrender (Sec. 80), a life policy is an indivisible contract for life, protected by statutory anti-forfeiture devices.

Grounds for Return of Remitted Premium Secs. 80–83
RETURN OF WHOLE PREMIUM
  • Contract voidable due to fraud/misrepresentation of insurer.
  • Rescission due to insurer's substantial breach.
  • Thing insured was never exposed to risk (e.g., voyage aborted).
  • Insured ignorant of facts without fault.
  • Insured's default without intentional fraud.
RETURN OF PRO-RATA PREMIUM
  • Surrender before term expiration in property insurance written for a definite period.
  • Over-insurance by double insurance: Proportional ratable refund of excess premium paid across underwriters.
  • Note: Pro-rata refund NEVER applies to life insurance.

8. Multiple Insurances: Double, Over-Insurance & Reinsurance (Secs. 95–100)

Imagine a commercial real estate mogul whose ₱1,000,000 building is insured for ₱500,000 each with four different companies (A, B, C, and D)—a total of ₱2,000,000 in coverage. A fire completely incinerates the building. How much can he collect, and who ultimately bears the loss?

Under Section 95, Double Insurance occurs when the same person is insured by several insurers separately in respect to the same subject and interest. It is valid per se. However, when total coverage exceeds the actual property value, it becomes Over-Insurance by Double Insurance, governed by the strict ratable loss settlement formula of Section 96.

Privity Comparison: Double Insurance vs. Reinsurance (Secs. 97–100)
Sec. 98: The original insured has no interest in the reinsurance contract and cannot sue the reinsurer directly.
⚠️ Examination Trap on "Other Insurance" Clause:
While Double Insurance is lawful per se, standard fire policies contain an Other Insurance Clause requiring disclosure of concurrent policies. The Supreme Court strictly holds that failure to disclose additional insurance constitutes a breach of warranty that completely avoids the policy!

PART III — LOSS, CLAIMS CONDITIONS & MARINE PERILS (Secs. 85–101)

9. Concept of Loss, Modes of Satisfaction & Proximate Cause (Secs. 85–89)

Imagine a vehicle policy covering flood, but excluding theft. A torrential typhoon submerges a car in deep floodwaters. While neighbors pull the car out of the water, opportunistic thieves sever the winch line and drive the car into a chop-shop compound. Does the insurer pay?

The insurer is liable where the proximate cause of the loss is a covered peril, even if the immediate cause was an excepted peril (Sec. 86). Under Section 87, losses incurred during rescue efforts from a covered peril are fully compensable!

Professor's Classroom Illustration on Proximate Cause: In the flooded car scenario, flood was the prime efficient cause that set the chain in motion without an independent break. Because the rescue was necessitated by the flood, the consequential theft during the rescue operation is chargeable to the underwriter!
Negligence vs. Willful Acts (Section 89) Liability Boundary
✓ SIMPLE NEGLIGENCE (Covered)

Carelessness, human lapses, or oversight by the insured or servants DO NOT exonerate the insurer. Protecting against simple negligence is a primary objective of buying insurance.

✗ GROSS NEGLIGENCE / FRAUD (Discharged)

Gross negligence amounting to reckless, willful misconduct, intentional destruction, or fraudulent arson discharges the insurer from all liability.

10. Notice of Loss, Proof of Loss & Beneficiary Status (Secs. 90–94)

Imagine an insured whose commercial building burns down. He orally informs the insurance agent 3 days later, but fails to submit formal sworn itemized inventory receipts for 3 months. Can the insurer escape liability if it initially denied the claim purely on the ground that the policy had expired?

Under Sections 93 and 94, any defect or delay in notice or proof of loss is deemed waived if the insurer fails to object promptly, or denies liability on other substantive grounds.

Statutory Rules on Notice & Proof of Loss
Requirement Statutory Rule Legal Consequence & Waiver
Notice of Loss (Sec. 90) Must be given without unnecessary delay in property insurance. Condition subsequent. Failure bars claim unless waived by insurer's conduct or failure to object (Sec. 93).
Proof of Loss (Secs. 91–92) Claimant must provide the best evidence available to substantiate loss. Does not require courtroom legal precision; reasonable documentation suffices. Delay caused by insurer is waived (Sec. 94).
Irrevocable Beneficiary Designation in life insurance where right to revoke is expressly waived. Acquires a vested property right. Insured cannot change beneficiary, take policy loans, or assign policy without consent.

11. Marine Insurance Scope: Perils of the Sea vs. Perils of the Ship (Sec. 101)

Imagine an ocean cargo freighter that sinks mid-voyage. In Scenario A, an unprecedented 50-foot rogue tidal wave capsizes the hull. In Scenario B, the ship's rusty wooden hull timbers slowly rot, allowing seawater to seep in during dead calm seas. Is the underwriter liable in both?

The core doctrinal pillar of marine insurance is that marine policies cover Perils of the Sea, NEVER Perils of the Ship.

The Great Maritime Dichotomy Sec. 101
PERILS OF THE SEA (Covered) PERILS OF THE SHIP (Excluded)
Fortuitous, extraordinary, catastrophic casualties caused by unusual violence of the wind and waves. Natural, normal, ordinary decay and inevitable wear and tear of navigation.
Violent typhoons, rogue waves, lightning, collisions without fault, stranding on uncharted reefs. Rusted plates, rotten timbers, unseaworthiness, engines running out of fuel, ordinary water seepage.
Rule: Insurer undertakes to protect against accidents of the sea. Rule: Insurer does NOT guarantee against the internal frailties of the vessel.
